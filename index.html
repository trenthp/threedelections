How to read the controls
Election
Picks the race. 2026 is a House midterm with lower turnout than a presidential year, so blocks are shorter and circles smaller.
Scale
How vote density turns into height. Linear is the most literal: block volume is proportional to votes, so a few big cities tower over everything. Square root tames the tallest counties so mid-sized ones are visible too. Log spreads out the smallest counties. Scale has no effect when Height is Flat 2D.
Color
Margin colors every county by how lopsided its result was, so large, sparse counties take up a lot of the map. Weighted fades sparsely populated counties toward gray, so color strength follows where people vote. Radial draws a circle in each county whose area is proportional to its votes, so the amount of color matches the amount of voters. Winner uses a solid party color, however close the race.
Exaggeration
Stretches every block's height by the same factor. It makes short counties easier to see without changing how they compare.
Height
Flat 2D is a standard map. Votes raises each county by votes cast per square mile. Net margin raises it by the winner's vote lead per square mile, so close races stay low even in big cities.
View
Camera presets. Drag to rotate, right-drag to pan, scroll to zoom, and press Space to switch between flat and 3D.

Height and color can both show how many people voted. For the most balanced picture, pair Votes height with Radial or Weighted color. For the familiar election-night map, use Flat 2D with Margin. The Land area and Votes bars above compare the two directly.

About the data
  • 2020 and 2024 county results: tonmcg/US_County_Level_Election_Results. Boundaries: U.S. Census Bureau via us-atlas. Land area is computed from each county's real geographic area, not its on-screen size.
  • 2026 is the U.S. House midterm (there's no presidential race), with every district's votes summed by county. These are unofficial counts from The New York Times and the Associated Press, compiled by mike10010100/elections-2026. Some counties are still counting, and counties with an uncontested seat show one-sided margins. Turnout is lower in midterms, so blocks are shorter than in presidential years.
  • Alaska reports results by state house district, which don't line up with boroughs, so Alaska is shown as one uniform statewide result.
  • Connecticut 2024 results are reported by the new planning regions. They're mapped onto the legacy counties with an approximate crosswalk.
  • Alaska and Hawaii are drawn as insets at a different scale, so their volumes aren't comparable with the lower 48.
  • 2016 is omitted because the available county file is a pre-certification snapshot whose totals don't match official results.
  • This is an independent visualization. It isn't affiliated with any campaign, party, news organization or election office.
